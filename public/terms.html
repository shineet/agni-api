What Agni does

Agni records what you eat, reads what Apple Health knows about your movement and sleep, and shows the two together with a daily food budget and some plain-language guidance. It works out food figures from photographs, from a bundled table of dishes, from public nutrition databases and from an AI model.

The figures are estimates

This is the most important thing to understand about the app, and Agni says so on the screen as well as here.

  • A photograph cannot measure. It cannot see the oil in a curry, how much ghee went on a roti, or what is underneath what. Agni makes an informed guess from what is visible and tells you when it is unsure.
  • The dish table is a reference, not your kitchen. Your mother's sambar is not the average sambar.
  • Apple Health figures are only as good as what recorded them. A phone in a bag counts no steps. A watch on a charger measures no heart rate.
  • The budget is arithmetic on top of estimates. It inherits every uncertainty underneath it.

Agni is designed to be useful for noticing patterns over weeks. It is not designed to be exact on any single plate, and treating one day's number as a fact is a misuse of it.

Your health is your responsibility

You decide what to eat and how to train. Agni is a tool for seeing your own behaviour more clearly, and nothing it shows is an instruction. If something it tells you contradicts your doctor, your doctor is right.

If any weight goal you set in Agni would be unhealthy for you, the app applies safety floors, but those floors are general and know nothing about you specifically. They are not a substitute for professional advice.
