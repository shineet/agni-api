Two estimates

Every plate gets estimated twice, independently

Agni estimates the dish as a whole, then estimates each ingredient on its own. Two readings of the same photograph, which is the only honest way to show you how soft the number really is.

It says outright that the two will not add up, and flags the assumptions doing the most damage, so you can correct the one that matters instead of all seven.

"kokum, not in the table, counted as nothing"When an ingredient is missing from its food table, it says so rather than inventing a figure.

On the wrist

The watch screen becomes the heart rate zone

Agni records the workout on the Apple Watch itself. Mid-stride, with a moving arm and sun on the glass, you are not going to read a small number in a corner, so the entire screen turns the colour of the zone you are in. Orange is threshold. Red is maximum. You know without focusing.

Underneath: the zone by name and its range, a bar showing which of the five you are in, your time and your calories. Afterwards the summary gives you splits, average and maximum heart rate, and the minutes you actually spent in each zone. Those workout calories then move your food budget, because the two halves are the same app.

Your own numbers

Your budget comes from your body, not from a 1990 equation

Most apps put your height, weight and age into an equation written in 1990 and hand you a number. Agni reads your own recent weeks from Apple Health and forecasts the whole day, so the figure is steady from the morning instead of climbing as you walk.

Workout days and regular days get different budgets, because your body does not spend the same on both. On a workout morning it asks you once which kind of day it is.

And it shows you the arithmetic. A diagnostics screen lists exactly which Health readings produced your number: how many valid days it found, the median resting energy, the median active energy on each kind of day. A budget you cannot check is a budget you are simply trusting.

The whole day

It is good at the food other trackers fumble

Home cooking, and South Asian food in particular, is where most calorie apps fall apart. The same curry made at home with a spoon of oil and in a restaurant finished with cream are not the same meal, and one entry for both is useless.

Sleep is read from Apple Health, so an Oura ring counts the same as a Watch or any app that writes there. Movement, workouts, protein and what you actually ate are all read together.

What comes back is not four dashboards. It is an assessment of the day with one thing worth doing about it, and it says when the picture is incomplete rather than scoring you on data it does not have.

What I am asking for

Twenty five people, honestly using it.

  • An iPhone. iOS 17 or later. An Apple Watch if you have one, because the watch app is half of this.
  • About a week of real days. Real meals, real workouts, real sleep. A week of ordinary living tells me more than a month of careful testing.
  • Tell me when it is wrong. A screenshot and what it actually was. That is the whole job.
  • A message when something confuses you. If a screen made no sense, that is a bug in my writing and I want to hear it.

Twenty five testers, before this goes on the App Store

It is finished enough to live with and rough enough that your complaints will still change it. That is the useful window, and it does not last long.
