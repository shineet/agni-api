Privacy Policy · SHAI Health, formerly Agni Log · Last updated 26 November 2026

Your food diary and your health data stay on your phone.

SHAI has no accounts and no sign-in, and it does not ask for your name. This page sets out what the app keeps on your phone, what it sends away and why, who receives it, and how long it is kept.

The short version.

  • Your food diary (every meal, weight, workout and everything SHAI reads from Apple Health) is stored on your iPhone, and the diary as a whole is never uploaded.
  • Five kinds of thing do leave your phone, and only when you use the feature that needs them:
    • a photo or food name you ask the AI to analyse;
    • a dish you create, which can be added to the shared dish table;
    • the name of a dish SHAI could not find;
    • a barcode you scan, which is looked up in Open Food Facts;
    • what you say or type to Aura, the AI assistant, with a short summary of today. The summary holds only your food log unless you choose to share your Apple Health data too.
  • AI analysis needs your permission first.
  • SHAI's server keeps usage counts and your subscription status so allowances work. Those records use identifiers that do not name you but do stay the same over time. I can link them to each other; I cannot link them to your name unless you tell me who you are.
  • There is no advertising, no tracking across other companies' apps or websites, and nothing is sold.

Who this is

SHAI is made by Shine Thankappan, an independent developer. There is no company behind it and no team. If you write to me, I read it myself.

What stays on your iPhone

SHAI's own storage on your device holds your complete diary:

  • every meal you log, with its dishes, calories and macros, and the small photo thumbnail SHAI keeps so you can recognise your meals;
  • everything SHAI reads from Apple Health (steps, energy burned, heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep and workouts);
  • your body details, weight, goals and targets;
  • your settings;
  • your conversations with Aura, in locked storage, for as long as you choose (see “Talking to Aura”);
  • if you use Teach Aura your voice, a small set of numbers that tunes how she recognises her name. The recordings themselves are not kept;
  • everything SHAI tells you, including “For you now”, recovery and movement assessments and workout notes. These are worked out on your phone.

None of this is uploaded as a diary. The specific pieces listed under “What leaves your device” are the only exceptions, and only when you use those features.

When you delete SHAI. Deleting the app removes what SHAI stores on your phone, with three exceptions:

  • Meals SHAI wrote to Apple Health stay in Health until you delete them there.
  • Your iPhone and iCloud backups may still contain SHAI's data until those backups are replaced.
  • Records on SHAI's server are not deleted by deleting the app. SHAI's install identifier is kept in the iPhone Keychain, which normally survives deleting and reinstalling the app, so reinstalling usually reconnects you to the same server records.

Apple Health

SHAI asks permission before reading anything, and you choose what to share. It reads steps, energy, heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep and workouts to show what you have burned against what you have eaten and to read your recovery. It writes the meals you log back to Health as dietary energy and macros, so other apps you use can see them.

Health data is read on your device and used on your device. It is not uploaded and never used for advertising. The one exception is Aura, and only if you choose it: when Aura first opens she asks whether to share your Health data with her. If you choose Continue and share my Health data, a short summary derived from it (today's guidance, movement totals, last night's sleep hours and your Recovery state) is sent with your question so she can answer. If you choose Continue with food only, none of it is sent. You can change the choice at any time with the heart button on Aura's screen, and it applies to your very next question. You can also withdraw any permission at any time in the Health app, under Sharing.

AI analysis, and your permission

  • Asking first. In SHAI 1.0 (build 180) and later, the first time you use an AI feature (a meal photo, a nutrition label, an AI food search or an ingredient lookup), SHAI shows a screen that names Anthropic, says what will be sent, and asks you to choose Allow or Not now. Nothing is sent unless you choose Allow.
  • Not now sends nothing. It is not remembered, so the next time you use an AI feature SHAI asks again.
  • Allow is remembered, and SHAI stops asking.
  • Turning it off. Settings > SHAI Pro > Allow AI analysis shows your choice. Switching it off does not block AI; it returns SHAI to asking before every AI use, and nothing is sent unless you choose Allow again.
  • Aura. The first time you open Aura she shows a screen that names Anthropic, says what will be sent and asks you to choose whether to share your Health data too; there is no question until you have chosen. Aura works only after that choice.
  • Without AI you can still log meals by hand, with search, barcode scanning and quick add.

Talking to Aura

Aura is an AI assistant, not a person and not a clinician. She cannot monitor your health or contact anyone, and a message about an emergency gets a fixed reply pointing to emergency services, with no AI involved.

  • Your voice stays on your iPhone. If you speak to Aura, your voice is turned into text on your iPhone only, using Apple's on-device speech recognition with no fallback to a network service. SHAI does not record or store the audio. Only the recognised words are sent, exactly as if you had typed them. Aura's replies are read aloud on your iPhone, with Apple's voice or, if you choose to download one, a more natural voice that also runs on the phone. Downloading a voice fetches its files from huggingface.co; nothing about you or your food log is sent.
  • When Aura is listening. SHAI 1.2 and later can wait for “Hey Aura” while SHAI is on screen, if you turn that on. The waiting is done on your iPhone by recognising the sound of her name; what is heard while waiting is discarded as it arrives. After an answer to a spoken question she keeps listening for about six seconds for a follow-up, and while she is speaking she can listen so that you can talk over her. All of it is on your iPhone, and iOS shows the microphone indicator whenever she is listening. SHAI does not listen while it is not on screen. If you set up Apple's Vocal Shortcuts to open Aura from elsewhere, it is Apple's feature that listens, under Apple's terms.
  • Questions answered on the phone. Some questions, such as what you ate or how much water you drank, are answered on your iPhone from your own records. Those questions and answers are not sent; later questions only carry a note that one was answered on the phone.
  • The conversation stays on your iPhone. It is kept in locked storage, unreadable while the phone is locked. In SHAI 1.2 and later each visit is a new conversation, and earlier ones are kept for 30 days unless you choose 7 days, a year, or not at all; you can delete any of them, or all of them, under the clock on Aura’s screen. In earlier versions the one conversation is kept until you tap New conversation. Deleting your data in Settings or deleting the app removes them. SHAI's server does not keep it. So that a follow-up question makes sense, the last few messages are sent with each new question.
  • Aura never changes your diary by herself. She can suggest a meal to log. The suggestion is priced on your iPhone from SHAI's own food table, and nothing is added until you tap Log or say yes. You can undo it. In SHAI 1.2 and later, asking her to log water adds it straight away, as you asked.
  • Free and Pro. Free accounts can ask Aura a few questions a day; SHAI Pro has a larger daily allowance. SHAI's server counts questions per day to apply this.

Snoring, mood and sharing a bed

These arrived in SHAI 1.3.

  • Snore tracking listens only when you start it. When you tap to start a night, SHAI records the sound of the night on your iPhone, in locked storage that is not included in backups. In the morning it works out where the snoring was, on your iPhone, and then deletes the night’s sound. It keeps what it found (when and how loudly you snored, and how sure it was moment to moment, but no sound) and, if you leave “Keep the loudest sounds” on, up to a dozen clips of about twelve seconds of snoring a night, never a moment in which someone spoke. Clips are kept for the period you choose and can be deleted one by one. Nothing about your nights is uploaded unless you share a bed with SHAI, below.
  • Sharing a bed. If you and your partner both track snoring with SHAI in the same bed, you can pair your two iPhones in Settings > Sleep > Sharing a bed: one iPhone shows a code and the other types it, so both of you have to agree. Each paired iPhone then sends SHAI’s server, for each night, the moments it was sure it heard a snore and how loud each one was compared with the quiet of the room. No sound, nothing that was said, and no cough or breathing readings. The other iPhone fetches them and decides, on the phone, which snores were yours and which your partner’s. The server deletes a night’s moments as soon as the other iPhone has compared with them, and after 7 days in any case. Stopping sharing on either iPhone deletes both iPhones’ nights from the server and ends the pairing.
  • Daily mood check-in. How you feel, and the energy, stress and context you add, are kept on your iPhone only. They are not sent to SHAI’s server or to Aura, and SHAI does not write them to Apple Health.
  • Mood from Apple Health. If you turn on “Import mood from Apple Health”, SHAI asks Health for permission to read your State of Mind entries, reads them on your iPhone and shows them with a label. It never writes to State of Mind, and nothing it reads is uploaded.

The companies involved

I do not sell your information. I share it only with the service providers below, only to run the features you use, and none of them receives your Apple Health records or your diary. The one thing that can reach Anthropic is the short Aura summary described above, and the Health-derived part of it only if you chose to share it.

  • Anthropic analyses photos, answers food questions and answers your questions to Aura. Requests reach Anthropic from SHAI's server with no identifier of yours attached. SHAI's server keeps Anthropic's identifier for each request in its usage record. Anthropic's commercial terms say it “may not train models on Customer Content”. SHAI uses Anthropic's Claude Sonnet and Claude Haiku models.
  • Vercel runs SHAI's server in its US East (Washington, D.C.) region.
  • Supabase holds SHAI's database in its US East (North Virginia) region: the dish table, the caches, usage records, subscription status and, if you asked for a beta invitation, your email address.
  • USDA FoodData Central answers some food lookups. It receives a food name from SHAI's server and nothing else.
  • Open Food Facts answers barcode lookups. Your phone sends it the barcode directly, with your IP address as any website receives it.
  • Apple processes your subscription payment, checks App Attest proofs, keeps the two DeviceCheck bits, and sends the server subscription notices. If you dictate a meal, your iPhone transcribes it on the device where it can, otherwise Apple's speech service does; SHAI's server never receives the audio. Apple's own privacy policy covers what Apple does.

Your rights, and how to use them

Your diary and Health data are on your own device, so you control them directly.

For what is kept on the server: write to me. Because there is no account, I can only act on records I can verify are yours.

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under the GDPR, the UK GDPR or the California Consumer Privacy Act to see, correct, export or delete what is held about you, to object to its use, and to complain to your data protection authority.

SHAI's server and database are in the United States. If you use SHAI from elsewhere, the limited information above is processed there.
