A Brighter You

Privacy Policy · Last updated 7 November 2026

Your food diary and your health data stay on your phone.

Agni has no accounts and no sign-in, and it does not ask for your name. This page sets out what the app keeps on your phone, what it sends away and why, who receives it, and how long it is kept.

The short version.

  • Your food diary (every meal, weight, workout and everything Agni reads from Apple Health) is stored on your iPhone, and the diary as a whole is never uploaded.
  • Three kinds of thing do leave your phone, and only when you use the feature that needs them:
    • a photo or food name you ask the AI to analyse;
    • a dish you create, which can be added to the shared dish table;
    • the name of a dish Agni could not find.
  • AI analysis needs your permission first.
  • Agni's server keeps usage counts and your subscription status so allowances work. Those records use identifiers that do not name you but do stay the same over time. I can link them to each other; I cannot link them to your name unless you tell me who you are.
  • There is no advertising, no tracking across other companies' apps or websites, and nothing is sold.

Who this is

Agni is made by Shine Thankappan, an independent developer. There is no company behind it and no team. If you write to me, I read it myself.

What stays on your iPhone

Agni's own storage on your device holds your complete diary:

  • every meal you log, with its dishes, calories and macros, and the small photo thumbnail Agni keeps so you can recognise your meals;
  • everything Agni reads from Apple Health (steps, energy burned, heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep and workouts);
  • your body details, weight, goals and targets;
  • your settings;
  • everything Agni tells you, including “For you now”, recovery and movement assessments and workout notes. These are worked out on your phone.

None of this is uploaded as a diary. The specific pieces listed under “What leaves your device” are the only exceptions, and only when you use those features.

When you delete Agni. Deleting the app removes what Agni stores on your phone, with three exceptions:

  • Meals Agni wrote to Apple Health stay in Health until you delete them there.
  • Your iPhone and iCloud backups may still contain Agni's data until those backups are replaced.
  • Records on Agni's server are not deleted by deleting the app. Agni's install identifier is kept in the iPhone Keychain, which normally survives deleting and reinstalling the app, so reinstalling usually reconnects you to the same server records.

Apple Health

Agni asks permission before reading anything, and you choose what to share. It reads steps, energy, heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep and workouts to show what you have burned against what you have eaten and to read your recovery. It writes the meals you log back to Health as dietary energy and macros, so other apps you use can see them.

Health data is read on your device and used on your device. It is not uploaded, not sent to any third party, and never used for advertising. You can withdraw any permission at any time in the Health app, under Sharing.

AI analysis, and your permission

  • Asking first. In Agni 1.0 (build 180) and later, the first time you use an AI feature (a meal photo, a nutrition label, an AI food search or an ingredient lookup), Agni shows a screen that names Anthropic, says what will be sent, and asks you to choose Allow or Not now. Nothing is sent unless you choose Allow.
  • Not now sends nothing. It is not remembered, so the next time you use an AI feature Agni asks again.
  • Allow is remembered, and Agni stops asking.
  • Turning it off. Settings > Agni Pro > Allow AI analysis shows your choice. Switching it off does not block AI; it returns Agni to asking before every AI use, and nothing is sent unless you choose Allow again.
  • Without AI you can still log meals by hand, with search, barcode scanning and quick add.

The companies involved

I do not sell your information. I share it only with the service providers below, only to run the features you use, and none of them receives your Apple Health data or your diary.

  • Anthropic analyses photos and answers food questions. Requests reach Anthropic from Agni's server with no identifier of yours attached. Anthropic's commercial terms say it “may not train models on Customer Content”. Agni uses Anthropic's Claude Sonnet and Claude Haiku models.
  • Vercel runs Agni's server in its US East (Washington, D.C.) region.
  • Supabase holds Agni's database in its US East (North Virginia) region: the dish table, the caches, usage records, subscription status and, if you asked for a beta invitation, your email address.
  • USDA FoodData Central answers some food lookups. It receives a food name from Agni's server and nothing else.
  • Apple processes your subscription payment, checks App Attest proofs, and sends the server subscription notices. Apple's own privacy policy covers what Apple does.

Your rights, and how to use them

Your diary and Health data are on your own device, so you control them directly.

For what is kept on the server: write to me. Because there is no account, I can only act on records I can verify are yours.

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under the GDPR, the UK GDPR or the California Consumer Privacy Act to see, correct, export or delete what is held about you, to object to its use, and to complain to your data protection authority.

Agni's server and database are in the United States. If you use Agni from elsewhere, the limited information above is processed there.
